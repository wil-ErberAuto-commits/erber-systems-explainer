How our systems fit together

Erber Auto is an independent repair shop in Moscow, Idaho. Behind the front counter there's one official record, one always-on office computer, and a small team of AI assistants. People make every decision that matters. Here's the whole picture, without the jargon.

The short version

Shop records start in Shopmonkey. The office PC keeps a local copy, cleans it up, and feeds the tools the team uses all day. AI assistants help build and run it all, and a person signs off on anything important.

One record, copied carefully

Shopmonkey is the shop's system of record: every customer, vehicle, and repair order lives there first.

ErberData runs on the office PC and keeps a local copy of those records, so office tools can look things up quickly without asking Shopmonkey every time. It also keeps parts-invoice records and retail parts prices.

The shop data model (we call it the ontology) reads a snapshot of that copy and turns it into one clean, trustworthy picture of customers, vehicles, and orders. It never writes back.

Status: the shop data model is built and reviewed, but it isn't switched on yet. It'll run nightly once it's been checked against real data.

Privacy rules built into the model

  • Masking. AI assistants only ever see masked views, never full customer details.
  • Shop texts (like "your car is ready") follow the permissions customers already gave in Shopmonkey.
  • Marketing uses the strictest answer. If we don't know a customer said yes, the answer is no.
  • Freshness checks flag data that's out of date instead of passing it off as current.

What runs on the office PC

If it runs at the shop, it runs on this one computer. Staff reach it by signing in with their work Google account.

Caller HUD
When the phone rings, the screen shows who's calling and their history with us. It can suggest call notes with AI, but an advisor approves each one.
Tagger
Workflow helpers for repair orders: tags orders, writes inspection summaries with Claude Haiku, and sends the pickup text when a car moves to Ready For Pickup.
O'Reilly price check
A fixed daily job that reads retail parts prices for quotes. No AI involved.
Systems page
A status board for the administrator: what's running, what needs a look, and a few safe, pre-approved restart buttons.

Also on the PC: parts-invoice intake (including a new phone scan page), a nightly backup, and the shop's knowledge notes.

AI assistants do the legwork. People decide.

A team of Grok Bot assistants helps build and run everything above. They draft, check, and prepare. Erber Grok leads the team and brings decisions to a person.

  • Developer writes and fixes code, and proposes changes for review.
  • Systems Ops runs a morning health check and handles safe restarts.
  • Marketing Lead plans marketing with the shop owner.
  • Researcher looks things up and checks facts.
  • Plus helpers for writing, design, email, meeting notes, and measuring results.

Only a person can

  • Merge code
  • Deploy to the office PC
  • Send anything to customers
  • Spend money

The shop owner also approves anything in his name, voice, or likeness.
